## Key insights

1. **Follower growth is essentially flat across every account except TikTok.** Four of five accounts moved less than 0.35% in 30 days — this is the number behind the CMO plan's own complaint about "recirculating content to the same audience." TikTok's +4.76% is the one real bright spot, consistent with it being a newly launched channel with room to grow. **Confidence: 5/5** on the numbers; the "why" (content strategy, posting cadence) isn't visible from this data alone.
2. **Post-launch engagement swings are large but not trustworthy as a launch effect** — the post-launch window is only 5–7 days, and several of the biggest percentage moves (+777%, +1,076%) are off tiny pre-launch bases (single-digit-to-low-double-digit daily averages), meaning one viral post or one quiet week would produce numbers like this with no connection to the website relaunch at all. **Confidence: 2/5** that any of these engagement shifts are actually caused by the July 1 launch — social content and website changes are different levers, and there's no reason a site relaunch would directly move Facebook/Instagram reach.
3. **The two accounts with the largest audiences (18K Facebook, 4,860 Instagram) both show declining views/reach post-launch**, which is the opposite direction from the smaller accounts. Without a content calendar cross-reference, this reads as normal week-to-week variance rather than a real signal — but it's the honest data, not a story to spin positive.
4. **This is a genuine first baseline, not a trend** — 30 days total, with only 5–7 of them post-launch. The right use of this data is as the starting point for the next report, not as a verdict on social performance today.
